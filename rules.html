Simple Present and Present Progressive

SIMPLE PRESENT

PAST ••• [NOW] ••• FUTURE

Expresses general truths, facts, and habits.

  • Water consists of hydrogen and oxygen. (General truth)
  • I get up at seven every morning. (Habit)

PRESENT PROGRESSIVE

PAST → [IN PROGRESS NOW] → FUTURE

Expresses a temporary activity happening right now.

  • The students are sitting at their desks right now.
  • I am taking five courses this semester.

Stative Verbs (Non-Progressive Verbs)

Stative verbs describe conditions or states that exist, not dynamic actions. They are rarely used in progressive tenses.

Mental State

know, believe, understand, recognize, remember, think, suppose

Emotional State

like, love, hate, dislike, fear, appreciate, want, prefer, need

Possession

possess, own, belong, contain, consist of

Sense Perception

taste, smell, hear, see, feel, look like, resemble

Dual-Meaning Examples:
  • I think that Mr. Liu is a good teacher. (State = believe)
  • I am thinking about my family right now. (Action = thoughts in mind)

Simple Past vs. Past Progressive

Simple Past

Indicates an activity that began and ended at a specific time in the past (e.g., I walked to school yesterday).

Past Progressive

Indicates an action that was in progress at a particular time in the past when another action occurred.

Example: Rita was standing under a tree when it began to rain.

Action 1 (In progress first): Standing under tree • Action 2 (Interrupted): It began to rain.

1-6

Unfulfilled Intentions: Was/Were Going To

Used to express past intentions or plans that were not fulfilled (did not happen).

Jack was going to go to the movie last night, but he changed his mind.

(Meaning: Jack planned to go, but he didn't go.)
